I'm a Principal Software Engineer who enjoys turning complex problems into software that makes a practical difference. I work across the full stack, primarily with TypeScript and .NET, helping teams make sound technical decisions and build systems that people can depend on.

My route into software began in mathematics. I studied semi-classical mechanics at master's level before completing a PhD in applied mathematics, where my research focused on mathematical biology and mammalian circadian rhythms. That background still shapes how I approach engineering: understand the system, question its assumptions and bring clarity to difficult problems.

I'm especially interested in public health and the role that thoughtful software and usable data can play in improving people's lives. I want to create applications that lead to better health outcomes, whether they support new research or help customers receive the clinical items they need, when they need them.

I've led engineering teams for more than six years. For me, principal engineering reaches beyond the code: it means connecting technical decisions to real outcomes, giving teams enough context to make good choices and creating a shared direction when the path is unclear. This blog is where I write about the technologies, ideas and problems I'm exploring, in an attempt to contribute to the wider engineering community.

I'm also a keen runner. I'll write about training, nutrition, new research and the events I've taken part in. Running and engineering reward many of the same habits: curiosity, consistency and a willingness to learn from what didn't go to plan.

Away from work and running, I'm vegan and care deeply about animal welfare. I also enjoy board games, good pubs and getting stuck into DIY projects, usually discovering another job hiding behind the one I started.
